# Module 3: Stakeholder Management & RTM

## Key Definitions
- **Project:** a *temporary* endeavor with a unique goal and usually a budget.
- **Project Management:** applying processes, methods, skills, knowledge, and experience to hit objectives within scope, time, cost, and quality, while meeting stakeholder expectations.

## Why PM Matters
1. **Improved efficiency:** better resource allocation, productivity, and cost savings
2. **Stakeholder satisfaction:** clear communication builds trust
3. **Organization & structure:** turns chaos into efficient processes
4. **Risk management:** identify, assess, and mitigate risks early
5. **Strategic goals:** projects drive organizational change

## What a PM Does
Planning (scope, schedule, resources, budget), Execution (leading the team), Communication (updating stakeholders), Risk Management, and Problem-Solving (handling conflicts and challenges).

## The 8 Project Performance Domains
A performance domain is a group of related activities critical for delivering project outcomes. The domains are interactive, interrelated, and interdependent, working in unison to achieve desired outcomes.

The 8 domains are interrelated areas that work together: **Stakeholders, Team, Development Approach & Life Cycle, Planning, Project Work, Delivery, Measurement, Uncertainty.**

## Stakeholder Performance Domain

The Stakeholder Performance Domain addresses activities and functions associated with stakeholders.

**Desired outcomes:**
- Productive working relationships with stakeholders throughout
- Stakeholder agreement with project objectives
- Beneficiaries are supportive; opponents don't negatively impact outcomes

**Definitions:**
- **Stakeholder:** anyone (person, group, organization) who may affect, be affected by, or *perceive* they're affected by the project.
- **Stakeholder Analysis:** systematically gathering info to decide whose interests to consider.
- **Stakeholder Management:** identify stakeholders, analyze expectations and impact, and plan how to involve them.

**Who they are (identified during Project Initiation, inner to outer circle):**
- Inner: Project Manager, PM Team, Project Team
- Middle: Governing Bodies, PMOs, Steering Committees
- Outer: Suppliers, Customers, End Users, Regulatory Bodies
- Also: top management, your manager, peers, resource managers, internal/external customers, government, contractors/subcontractors, and others (public, landowners, interest groups, competitors)

**Stakeholder Management Tools:**

| Tool | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Power-Interest Matrix | Enhance project management efficiency and foster better relationships with stakeholders |
| Cooperation-Threat Matrix | Develop more effective stakeholder engagement strategies and enhance project success |
| Stakeholder Register | Lists of stakeholders and stakeholder groups |
| Stakeholder Analysis Template | Enhance project management, improve stakeholder relationships, and increase the likelihood of project success |
| Communication Plan | Crucial for the successful management of projects, ensuring that everyone involved is informed and engaged |

**Stakeholder Register:** a living table used throughout the project. It tracks interests, involvement, interdependencies, influence, all interactions (planned or not), and who on the team is responsible. Closely tied to the communication plan.

**Power/Interest Grid:**

| | Low Interest | High Interest |
|---|---|---|
| **High Power** | Keep Satisfied | Manage Closely |
| **Low Power** | Monitor | Keep Informed |

Questions to ask: Who are they? What are their interests? Will interest change over time? Can coalitions be built?

**Cooperation-Threat Matrix:**

| | Low Threat | High Threat |
|---|---|---|
| **Low Cooperation** | Marginal → Monitor | Non-Supportive → Defend |
| **High Cooperation** | Supportive → Involve | Mixed Blessing → Collaborate |

**Relationship Building (steps):** Analyze stakeholders → Assess influence → Understand expectations → Define success → Keep stakeholders involved → Keep stakeholders informed.

**Project Sponsor** (source: PMBOK Guide, 5th Edition, p. 32): the person or group responsible for enabling success, may be inside but usually outside the project, **signs off on completion** (the one the PM must satisfy), escalates issues beyond the PM's control, and helps create the charter and plan.

**Politics & relationships:** Supportive stakeholders are essential. Consider the environment, each group's goals (stated and hidden agendas), and power. Build respect by being honest, taking ownership, being reliable, standing by decisions, and owning mistakes. Cultural differences affect communication, negotiation, and decision-making.

## Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM)
A document that tracks every requirement through design, development, and testing.

**Purpose:** track requirements, ensure full coverage, support change management.

**Typical columns:** Requirement ID, Description, Source, Related Test Cases, Design/Implementation Details, Verification Status, Priority.

**Types:**
- **Forward:** requirement → design → code → tests
- **Backward:** every product element traces back to a requirement
- **Bi-directional:** both, so changes ripple correctly either way

**Benefits:** requirement compliance, defect prevention, project visibility, easier audits.

**When to use:** requirement analysis, design, testing/validation, maintenance/upgrades.

**Tools:** Excel; JIRA, HP ALM, IBM DOORS; Trello, Confluence with add-ons.

**Challenges:** complexity at scale, frequent updates, manual errors.

**Best practices:** start early, review with stakeholders, automate where possible.

### RTM Exercise (Online Learning Platform)
**Learning outcomes.** By completing the exercise, you should be able to:
- Understand the purpose and structure of an RTM
- Track requirements across design, development, and testing stages
- Recognize how RTMs support project quality by linking requirements to deliverables and ensuring accountability
- Appreciate the role of RTMs in managing changes, tracking progress, and verifying all project requirements

**Scenario:** a university platform for course materials, quizzes, grades, instructor communication, progress tracking, and data privacy compliance.

**Steps:**
1. **Identify requirements** and split into functional and non-functional (e.g., User Login, Course Material Access, Quiz Functionality, Data Privacy Compliance)

5. **Track status** as work progresses (coded but untested = In Progress)
6. **Analyze gaps** for missing design, dev, or test steps, and adjust for new or changed requirements

# Module 4: Project Selection & Chartering

**Audience:** 3rd/4th year IT students, CICS.

## Learning Objectives
- **Master selection criteria:** evaluate and select projects using financial criteria (Payback Period, ROI, NPV) and strategic drivers (CILO1)
- **Framework context:** understand the PMBOK Initiating Process Group and how Project Integration Management bridges strategy and execution (CILO3)
- **Authorize initiatives:** build a professional Project Charter that sets boundaries and officially grants the PM authority
- **Real-world application:** connect charter goals to real technology architectures like Cloud Migrations and Digital KYC apps

## Core Idea
- **Project Selection ("speed dating for ideas"):** IT has limited budget, time, and developers but many ideas. Leadership picks the one with the most business value.
- **Project Charter ("birth certificate & contract"):** officially creates the project, states why it exists, and **authorizes the PM** to assign resources and spend funds.

## Initiating Workflow
**Business Need** (problem or opportunity) → **Selection Criteria** (financial and non-financial filters) → **Business Case** (cost vs. benefit proposal) → **Project Charter** (authorization and PM assignment)

## Financial Selection Criteria
- **Payback Period** = Initial Investment ÷ Annual Net Cash Inflow
- **ROI** = (Net Profit ÷ Total Cost) × 100
- **NPV:** total expected gain with future cash flows discounted to present value

## Non-Financial Drivers (can override ROI)
- **Regulatory compliance** (e.g., Central Bank AML laws, Data Privacy)
- **System reliability** (avoiding catastrophic downtime)
- **Competitive necessity** (matching industry baselines)
- **Social responsibility** (SDG goals like decent work and economic growth)

## Case: EduTech Solutions

| Factor | A: Cloud Migration | B: Mobile QR Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Initial Cost | $150,000 | $50,000 |
| Payback | 2.5 years | 1.0 year |
| ROI | 15% | 35% |
| Strategic Goal | Stability & SLA compliance | Market differentiation & growth |
| Main Risk | Migration downtime | Low faculty adoption |

**Outcome:** B has the better ROI, but **A was chosen** because stability during exam weeks is non-negotiable.

## 6 Key Elements of a Charter
1. **Project Purpose:** the business problem or opportunity
2. **SMART Objectives:** specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound
3. **Scope Boundaries:** high-level deliverables **and explicit exclusions**
4. **Key Constraints:** deadlines, tech limits, funding caps
5. **Summary Milestones:** high-level timeline of check-ins and phases
6. **PM Authority:** decision-making and financial thresholds

## Case: Zenith Bank Digital KYC
- **Problem:** 40% customer drop-off from a 3-day manual account process
- **Project:** a "10-Minute Digital KYC Onboarding Module" using OCR and facial recognition
- **Constraints:** AML laws, third-party biometric API dependency, legacy overnight batch processing

**Sample charter:**
- **Purpose:** cut drop-off from 40% to under 10% by implementing real-time identity verification and automated data capture
- **Success criteria:** account creation under 10 minutes; handles 5,000 concurrent verifications
- **Constraints:** budget capped at $200,000; non-negotiable 6-month go-live before Q4
- **PM authority:** can reallocate up to 10% of contingency funds and approve schedule changes under 5 days without board review

## Charter Pitfalls
- ❌ **Too high-level:** a "blank check" that invites scope creep
- ❌ **Too detailed:** putting a WBS in the charter forces constant rework
- ✅ **Balanced:** high-level goals, clear constraints, defined PM thresholds

> **Key line:** A charter is NOT a project plan. The Charter authorizes **WHAT** and **WHY**. The RTM and WBS define **HOW**.

**Discussion prompt:** The sponsor says: *"Do not spend time writing explicit exclusions or out-of-scope items in the charter. We will figure out what we don't want as we build the software."*
- What specific **technical and financial** risks come from following this advice?
- How would you professionally explain to the sponsor that out-of-scope items protect the budget?

## Charter Assignment Checklist (Weeks 4 to 5 Summative Assessment)
- **Key Personnel:** project title, executive sponsor, designated PM
- **Strategic Value:** business purpose linked to organizational strategy
- **Targets:** SMART objectives, quantifiable success criteria
- **Boundaries:** high-level scope, deliverables, constraints, risks
- **Empowerment:** PM authority level signed off by the sponsor

**Next topic:** Weeks 6 to 7, Project Stakeholder Management.

# Module 5: WBS & WBS Dictionary

**Why it matters:** Week 8 scope management topic (a required PMBOK core competency for IT projects), tested in **Long Test 2**, and required for the final **Project Management Plan**.

## Learning Objectives
- Understand how the WBS sets the baseline for scope control
- Apply the **100% Rule** (no gaps, no overlaps)
- Build a WBS Dictionary with acceptance criteria and owners
- Tell apart deliverable-based WBS items from task-based activity lists

## What a WBS Is
**The unstructured scenario:** imagine telling 5 engineers to "Build a University Student Portal" with no clear decomposition:
- Engineer A codes login screens without backend API specs
- Engineer B designs tuition payment databases independently
- Engineer C configures cloud infrastructure with unverified tools

Result: chaos, overlapping effort, missed requirements, and severe schedule slip.

A **WBS** breaks the project into small, **deliverable-oriented** pieces called **Work Packages**, so everyone knows exactly what's in scope.

## Scope Baseline = 3 Parts
1. **Approved Scope Statement:** scope, major deliverables, assumptions, constraints
2. **WBS:** hierarchical breakdown of all work
3. **WBS Dictionary:** descriptions, acceptance criteria, cost, and owners per package

> **100% Rule (Golden Rule):** The WBS covers 100% of the scope, including internal, external, and PM work. No more, no less.

## Example WBS: Cloud Banking Application Migration
```
1.0 Cloud Banking Application Migration
├── 1.1 Project Management & Governance
│   ├── 1.1.1 Project Charter & Planning Documents
│   └── 1.1.2 Stakeholder Communication Reports
├── 1.2 User Authentication Module
│   ├── 1.2.1 Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) Engine
│   └── 1.2.2 OAuth2 Single Sign-On Integration
├── 1.3 Core Financial Services
│   ├── 1.3.1 Account Balance API
│   └── 1.3.2 Real-time Fund Transfer Engine  [Work Package]
└── 1.4 Cloud Infrastructure & Security
    ├── 1.4.1 AWS Production Environment Provisioning
    └── 1.4.2 Database Encryption & PCI-DSS Compliance
```

**What each node covers (from the diagram slide):**

| Code | Node | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1.1 | Project Charter & Planning Documents | Core scope baseline & charter deliverables |
| 1.1.2 | Stakeholder Communication Reports | Governance & status reporting cadence |
| 1.2.1 | MFA Engine | Security token & biometric auth |
| 1.2.2 | OAuth2 Single Sign-On Integration | Identity provider integration |
| 1.3.1 | Account Balance API | Real-time balance lookup service |
| 1.3.2 | Real-time Fund Transfer Engine | Work package (lowest-level deliverable, see dictionary) |
| 1.4.1 | AWS Production Environment Provisioning | Cloud infrastructure setup |
| 1.4.2 | Database Encryption & PCI-DSS Compliance | Compliance & data protection |

## Example Dictionary Entry (1.3.2)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Code & Name | 1.3.2 Real-time Fund Transfer Engine |
| Parent | 1.3 Core Financial Services |
| Description | Design, build, and integrate RESTful microservices for interbank transfers using ISO 20022 message formats |
| Owner | Lead Backend Engineer (Fintech Core Team) |
| Acceptance Criteria | (1) Latency under 1.5 s per transaction at peak load; (2) 100% ISO 20022 audit logging compliance; (3) zero critical vulnerabilities in static and dynamic scans |
| Effort / Cost | 60 hours / $9,000 USD |

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

**1. The Task-List Trap (verbs instead of nouns)**
- ❌ 1.1 Write Python Code, 1.2 Test Code, 1.3 Fix Bugs (ongoing actions, can't confirm completion)
- ✅ 1.1 Auth Microservice, 1.2 Test Execution Report, 1.3 Staging Deployment (clear, acceptable outputs)

**2. Wrong sizing**
- **Over-decomposed (under 8 hours):** massive admin overhead, micromanagement fatigue, tracking complexity
- **Under-decomposed (over 80 hours):** creates "blind spots" where cost overruns and schedule delays stay hidden until too late
- *(Implied, not stated on the slide: aim for work packages between 8 and 80 hours.)*

## Waterfall vs. Agile Scope

| Factor | Predictive (Waterfall WBS) | Adaptive (Agile Backlog) |
|---|---|---|
| Hierarchy | WBS → Control Account → Work Package | Epic → Feature → User Story |
| Timing | All upfront before execution | Iterative (backlog refinement) |
| Scope Control | Formal Change Request via Change Control Board | Backlog re-prioritized each sprint |
| Best For | Fixed scope, regulated, infrastructure | Evolving products, mobile apps, SaaS |

## Case: QuickMart Scope Creep
- **Context:** grocery app, 4-month hard deadline, $120,000 budget
- **Problem:** Week 3, Marketing informally asks for an AI recommendation engine, and the lead dev starts building it
- **PM response:** (1) check the WBS: it's not there; (2) flag it as scope creep; (3) freeze the work and file a formal Change Request showing the impact (+$18k, +3 weeks) before approval

## Student Challenge (flawed WBS)
```
1.0 Campus Event Management System
├── 1.1 Talk to Student Council Representatives
├── 1.2 Design Database Schema & ERD
├── 1.3 Write Backend Code in Node.js
└── 1.4 Perform Integration Testing
```
Problem: every item uses an action verb (Talk, Design, Write, Perform). Tasks: identify at least two errors, rewrite Level 2 as nouns, and write one testable acceptance criterion for a new deliverable.

## Takeaways
- Scope Baseline = Scope Statement + WBS + WBS Dictionary
- Structure around **outputs (nouns)**, not actions (verbs)
- **If it's not in the WBS, it's out of scope**
- Next: Week 9, Project Quality Management (quality planning, QA vs. QC, 7 basic quality tools)

# Module 7: Project Quality Management

**From Scope Baselines to Operational Excellence:** concepts, terminology, analytical tools and case analysis. Scheduled for Weeks 10 to 11.

**Core focus:** QA vs. QC and Cost of Quality. **Analytics:** the 7 Basic Quality Tools and DMAIC. **Decision making:** the Targeted Quality Gate strategy.

## From Requirements to Quality

| | Requirements Traceability (RTM) | Project Quality Management |
|---|---|---|
| **What it does** | Feature mapping: maps stakeholder needs directly to functional code components | Performance standard: evaluates stability, reliability, and stress limits under load |
| **Core question** | "What features must we build?" | "How well must those features perform?" |
| **Measure** | Completeness metric: 100% Functional Coverage | User satisfaction: Zero Systemic Outages |

> **Key takeaway:** meeting 100% of RTM features while crashing under peak load is a **critical quality failure**.

## Quality vs. Grade

| | Quality | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Keyword** | CONFORMANCE | COMPLEXITY |
| **Definition** | The degree to which a set of inherent characteristics fulfills explicit and implicit requirements reliably | A category or technical rank given to deliverables sharing the same functional use but varying feature complexity |
| **Example** | A lightweight command-line utility executing without crashes has high quality | A basic open-source text editor vs. a fully featured enterprise IDE with AI assistants |

> **Golden Rule:** Low grade is acceptable if planned. Low quality is never acceptable.

## Quality Assurance (QA) vs. Quality Control (QC)

| | Quality Assurance (QA) | Quality Control (QC) |
|---|---|---|
| **Orientation** | PROCESS-oriented | PRODUCT-oriented |
| **Focus** | Defect **Prevention** | Defect **Detection** |
| **Activities** | **Coding Standards:** establishing clean code conventions | **Execution Testing:** running automated unit and integration tests |
| | **CI/CD Pipelines:** automating deployment and review gates | **Security & Load Audits:** penetration testing and benchmarking |
| | **Architecture Guidelines:** enforcing scalable system designs | **User Acceptance:** conducting formal UAT sign-offs |

## Cost of Quality (CoQ) Framework

| Category | Cost type | IT examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost of Conformance** (money spent to avoid failure) | **1. Prevention Costs** | Code reviews and developer training; automated test script creation |
| | **2. Appraisal Costs** | Security audits and vulnerability scans; penetration testing and load testing |
| **Cost of Non-Conformance** (money spent because of failure) | **3. Internal Failure Costs** | Rework and emergency bug fixes; re-testing overhead before release |
| | **4. External Failure Costs** | System downtime and production outages; SLA penalties, lost sales and lawsuits |

> **Takeaway:** investing in Prevention (QA) always costs less than External Failures.

## The 7 Basic Quality Tools in IT

| Purpose | Tools |
|---|---|
| **Root Cause Analysis** | **Fishbone / Ishikawa Diagram:** categorizes defects by origin across system layers |
| **Priority & Tallying** | **Pareto Charts** (80/20 Rule), **Histograms**, and **Check Sheets**, for bug prioritization |
| **Process Control** | **Scatter Diagrams**, **Flowcharts**, **Run Charts**, and **Control Charts**, for operational stability |

## Fishbone & Pareto Analysis
1. **Fishbone (Ishikawa) Diagram:** categorizes causes across **People, Infrastructure, Code Architecture, and Dependencies** to locate API latency root causes.
2. **Pareto Chart (80/20 Rule):** shows that **80% of system downtime originates from 20% of critical defects** (e.g., unindexed queries and memory leaks).

## Control Charts & the Seven Run Rule
**Process boundary thresholds:** a control chart tracks system behavior (e.g., API response times) over sequential build iterations, using statistical standard deviation boundaries (**±3σ**).

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **UCL** | Upper Control Limit, +3σ |
| **μ** | Process Mean, the baseline |
| **LCL** | Lower Control Limit, −3σ |

**The Seven Run Rule:** if **7 consecutive data points fall on one side of the mean**, even inside the control limits, the process is **out of control** and needs immediate investigation, due to systemic bias.

**Actionable insight:** it signals non-random variation, such as **memory degradation** or **background process strain**.

## Six Sigma & DMAIC
**Six Sigma operational target:** **≤ 3.4 Defects Per Million Opportunities (DPMO).**

| Phase | Step | In IT |
|---|---|---|
| **D** | Define | CTQ specifications |
| **M** | Measure | Baseline metrics |
| **A** | Analyze | Isolate process flaws |
| **I** | Improve | Refactor & automate |
| **C** | Control | Prevent regression |

*(CTQ is the standard Six Sigma abbreviation for "Critical to Quality"; the slide uses only the abbreviation.)*

## Risk vs. Issue (Future Uncertainty vs. Present Reality)

| | Project Risk | Project Issue |
|---|---|---|
| **Probability** | **< 100%** | **= 100%** |
| **Definition** | An uncertain event impacting project scope, cost, or schedule, **positively or negatively** | An active problem happening **right now**, threatening scope, cost, or delivery time |
| **IT example** | A third-party API's latency **might** spike during Black Friday | A third-party API **crashed** at 2:00 AM; transactions are failing |
| **PM action** | **Proactive:** mitigation, contingency planning, risk buffers | **Reactive:** incident management, workarounds, escalation |

> **Professor's insight:** Beginners manage issues; senior PMs manage risks. Constant fire-fighting means your risk management plan failed 3 sprints ago.

## Iterative Risk Management Lifecycle (PMBOK)
A **continuous** process:

1. **Plan Risk Management:** define tools, team roles, and the risk budget
2. **Identify Risks:** brainstorming, SWOT analysis, Delphi technique
3. **Perform Qualitative Analysis:** assign Probability (P) and Impact (I) ratings; calculate **Score = P × I**
4. **Plan Risk Responses:** select a strategy: Avoid, Mitigate, Transfer, or Accept
5. **Monitor & Control Risks:** continuous tracking with the **Top 10 items list**, and audit residual risks across iterations

## Standard IT Risk Register Template

| Risk ID | Category | Risk Event & Description | Root Cause / Trigger | Risk Owner | Response Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | Technical | DB failover sync latency > 500ms SLA | High read/write throughput during peak load | Lead Architect | **Mitigate:** Redis cache layer |
| R-02 | Organizational | Lead DevOps engineer resigns mid-deployment | Competitive tech market; talent poaching | Eng Manager | **Mitigate:** Cross-train; CI/CD docs |
| R-03 | External | Payment vendor deprecates API v2 endpoint early | Vendor product lifecycle change | Integration PM | **Transfer/Avoid:** Migrate to v3 |

## Qualitative Risk Matrix & Exposure Score
**Exposure Score = Probability × Impact (P × I)**, each rated 1 to 5 (Impact: 1 = Minor to 5 = Critical).

| P ↓ \ I → | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
| **4** | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |
| **3** | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 | 15 |
| **2** | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
| **1** | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |

| Zone | Scores | Level | Required action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Red** | 15 to 25 | Critical | Immediate mitigation and executive visibility. Stop work if unmitigated. |
| 🟡 **Yellow** | 8 to 12 | Medium | Active monitoring and planned contingency triggers |
| 🟢 **Green** | 1 to 6 | Low | Keep on a passive watch list; re-evaluate periodically |

*(The zones skip 7, 13 and 14, but no P × I product can be 7, 13 or 14, so every cell still lands in a zone.)*

## 4 Core Threat Response Strategies

| Strategy | Tagline | What it means | IT example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Avoid** | Change path | Eliminate the threat by changing scope or technical implementation | Descope a risky microservice before release |
| **Mitigate** | Reduce impact | Reduce probability or impact before the failure occurs | Add a Redis cache layer and automated tests |
| **Transfer** | Shift burden | Shift the financial or operational burden to a third party via contract | Cloud provider uptime insurance and SLAs |
| **Accept** | Reserve / watch | Acknowledge the risk without changing project plans | Reserve a $50k financial contingency fund |
