# Module 5: Financial Planning

> **Key line:** a technopreneurship financial plan outlines the pathway to **commercializing a technological solution** while **rigorously managing capital**.

## Formulas

### Gross Burn Rate
- **Formula:** Gross Burn Rate = total cash spent per month
- **What it tells you:** the pure cash outflow required to keep the tech running, before any revenue comes in.
- **Example:** 250,000 + 80,000 + 45,000 + 15,000 + 10,000 = **400,000**

### Net Burn Rate
- **Formula:** Net Burn Rate = Gross Burn Rate − Monthly Revenue
- **What it tells you:** the actual net amount of cash the business loses each month. It reveals true account depletion, which is why investors look at it.
- **Example:** 400,000 − 150,000 = **250,000 / month**

### Cash Runway
- **Formula:** Cash Runway = Cash Balance ÷ Net Burn Rate
- **What it tells you:** how many months the startup can survive, the time left to hit profitability or raise the next funding round. Use the **net** burn rate, not the gross.
- **Example:** 5,000,000 ÷ 250,000 = **20 months**

### Contribution Margin
- **Formula:** Contribution Margin = Selling Price − Variable Cost
- **What it tells you:** the money left over from each unit or subscription after its direct delivery costs, which goes toward chipping away at fixed overhead.
- **Example:** 2,500 − 500 = **2,000 / user / month**

### Break-Even Point (Units)
- **Formula:** Break-Even Point (Units) = Total Fixed Monthly Costs ÷ Contribution Margin per Unit
- **What it tells you:** the exact unit volume needed to reach zero net loss and cover all fixed overhead: the minimum number of active users. Fixed costs are the numerator, contribution margin the denominator.
- **Example:** 300,000 ÷ 2,000 = **150 subscribers**

### Break-Even Sales Revenue
- **Formula:** Break-Even Sales Revenue = Subscribers × Price per Unit
- **What it tells you:** the same break-even point expressed as money, the monthly sales revenue required to break even.
- **Example:** 150 × 2,500 = **375,000 / month**

### Total Pro Forma Investment
- **Formula:** Total Pro Forma Investment = Fixed Capital Assets + Pre-Operating Expenses + Working Capital Margin
- **What it tells you:** the exact funding ask for investors: the CapEx, plus one-time startup costs, plus a reserve so the company doesn't go bankrupt in Month 1.
- **Example:** 1,280,000 + 200,000 + 1,020,000 = **2,500,000**

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Financial Plan** | An overview of current business financials and projections for growth |
| **Burn Rate** | Monthly speed at which seed capital is spent before positive cash flow |
| **Runway** | How many months a startup can survive |
| **CAC & LTV** | Customer Acquisition Cost versus total Lifetime Value per user |
| **ARR / MRR** | Annual / Monthly Recurring Revenue (baseline subscription income) |
| **CapEx** | Capital Expenditure: the technology infrastructure, hardware and IP needed to launch |
| **Pre-Operating Expenses** | One-time startup costs (security audit, permits, branding) |
| **Working Capital Margin** | Operating runway reserve, so the company doesn't go bankrupt in Month 1 |
| **Pro Forma Statements** | Forward-looking 3-to-5 year projections built by **bottom-up** forecasting |
| **COGS (for tech)** | Includes hosting costs and customer support operations |
| **Fixed Overhead Costs** | Costs that stay the same whether there is 1 user or 1,000 |
| **Contribution Margin** | Net revenue per unit after direct variable costs of delivery |
| **Payoff Threshold** | The user volume or time where unit margins fully offset fixed overhead |
| **Non-dilutive** | Funding that takes no ownership share (the government grant, 8%) |

## Lists to Know
- **A financial plan prepares you to:** pitch to investors, receive funding, achieve long-term success.
- **Its two key components:** current monetary situation, future growth projections.
- **Technopreneurship focus areas:** heavy upfront R&D, scalable tech, pricing models (SaaS), VC timelines.
- **Three components of the financial plan:**
  1. **CapEx & Pro Forma Investments:** R&D and prototyping, tech stack and infrastructure, intellectual property.
  2. **Pro Forma Financial Statements:** Income Statement (profitability), Cash Flow Statement (timing gaps), Balance Sheet (position at a point in time).
  3. **Break-Even Analysis:** when unit margins offset fixed technical overhead.
- **Pro forma investment parts:** fixed capital assets, pre-operating expenses, working capital margin.
- **Capital sourcing example:** Founders' Equity 20% (500,000), Angel/VC 72% (1,800,000), Government Grant 8% non-dilutive (200,000).

# Module 6: Production Planning and Control

> **Key lines:** the goal of technopreneurial PPC is to deploy evolving tech **without running out of cash before achieving Product-Market Fit**. "In technopreneurship, **cash is the ultimate constraint**."

## Formula
> **C2C Cycle = DIO + DSO − DPO** (also called the Cash Conversion Cycle, CCC)

**What it tells you:** the number of days it takes to turn cash spent on raw materials or manufacturing labor back into cash from customer sales, that is, how long capital stays trapped before it returns as revenue. The shorter, the better for a startup's runway. Inventory and receivables add days; paying suppliers later takes days off.

| Part | Stands for | Effect | Days that... |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DIO** | Days Inventory Outstanding | + added | parts and goods sit in the warehouse or factory before being sold |
| **DSO** | Days Sales Outstanding | + added | it takes to collect cash from customers after a sale |
| **DPO** | Days Payable Outstanding | − subtracted | the startup takes to pay its suppliers and contract manufacturers |

- **Traditional PPC:** long C2C, cash trapped for **90 to 120+ days**.
- **Technopreneurship PPC:** **short or negative** C2C. Low DIO (Just-In-Time), deferred supplier payments (higher DPO).

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **PPC** | Production Planning and Control: turns a prototype or code architecture into a scalable, commercially viable product |
| **DFM** | Design for Manufacturing: how a design scales from one bench prototype to thousands of units |
| **JIT** | Just-In-Time: minimal buffer stock, parts arrive right before assembly |
| **OEE** | Overall Equipment Effectiveness: traditional PPC's key metric (idle machine = lost money) |
| **BOM** | Bill of Materials. In a startup it is **alive** and changes with parts availability |
| **ECO** | Engineering Change Order: a mid-production design change |
| **PLM** | Product Lifecycle Management, cloud tools like **OpenBOM** or **Propel** |
| **HIL** | Hardware-in-the-Loop testing, with jigs ("test beds") on the assembly line |
| **CM** | Contract Manufacturer (Tier 1 to Tier 3) |
| **BYOP** | "Bring Your Own Parts": procure long-lead or custom silicon yourself, ship to the assembler |
| **MOQ** | Minimum Order Quantity: higher unit cost versus tying up survival capital |
| **NTC** | National Telecommunications Commission: certifies any Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or cellular device in the Philippines |

## Traditional vs. Technopreneurship PPC

| | Traditional | Technopreneurship |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Minimize unit cost (Economies of Scale) | Maximize learning rate and speed-to-market (Agile Validation) |
| **Measure of success** | Unit cost and OEE | Time-to-market and cash runway preservation |
| **Production** | Massive runs to dilute fixed costs | Micro-batches (3D printing, CNC, flexible cloud software) |
| **Tooling** | Heavy upfront (steel molds) | Soft, flexible (3D printing, CNC machining) |
| **Inventory** | Asset or buffer, bought in bulk | Liability or risk, kept tight (JIT) |
| **Capacity** | High utilization, 90%+ | Excess or fluid, modular setups |
| **Supply chain** | Rigid, long-term contracts | Hybrid, fluid contract manufacturing |
| **Nightmare** | A machine breakdown | The wrong product (6 months mass-producing what nobody buys) |
| **Quality control** | Static, end-of-line sampling, frozen design | Agile, continuous, in-line, design evolves via ECOs |

## Lists to Know
- **Production Planning (the architect):** Agile Demand Forecasting (pre-orders, crowdfunding, pilot signals), Design for Manufacturing, Routing & Sourcing (make in-house vs. contract out).
- **Production Control (the real-time radar):** Shop Floor & Micro-batch, Dynamic Inventory Tracking (JIT), Bottleneck & Burn-Rate.
- **Three core pillars:**
  1. **Dynamic BOM Management**
  2. **Modular Stage Scaling:** Alpha/Beta (single hand-assembled units), Pilot Run (**10 to 100 units**), Mass Customization (global contract partner)
  3. **Cash Runway Alignment:** match material drops to investor funding tranches. Parts delivered 3 months early cause the **Capital Freeze Risk**.
- **Sourcing mix (Make vs. Buy vs. Hack):** CM Selection (Tier 1 vs. Tier 3), BYOP, the MVP Ecosystem (**PCBWay** for electronics, **Protolabs** for injection molding).
- **Transition path:** launch at **Tier 3** (low risk, engineering collaboration), then scale to **Tier 2/1** after product-market fit. Starting at Tier 1 is almost always a mistake.
- **Supply chain financial levers:** C2C Cycle, MOQ Strategy, Component Obsolescence (modular BOM).
- **Three QC tactics and their steps:**
  1. **HIL Assembly Testing:** Clamped, Flashed & Tested, On-Spot Rejection (before permanent housing).
  2. **Dynamic ECOs:** Instant Push, Floor Sync, Zero Halts.
  3. **Post-Purchase Telemetry:** Data Transmit, Issue Detect, PPC Update.
- **Local (Philippines):** DOST-AMCen, DTI FabLab for 3D-printed jig enclosures, manufacturers in Laguna or Cavite, NTC certification, pre-certified RF modules like ESP32.
